Why tanh is defined as following?

$$
\tanh(\phi) = \frac{e^\phi - e^{-\phi}}{e^\phi + e^{-\phi}}
$$

A hyperbola is the set of all points where the absolute difference of distances to two fixed points (foci) is constant.

$$
\lVert PF_1 - PF_2 \rVert = constant
$$

base on this definition and after a tedious algebraic expansion and elimination, you can obtain a nice analytic form.

$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
$$

Just like to define trigonometry over unit circle,
hyperbolic function can be defined over unit hyperbola.

$$
x^2 - y^2 = 1 \tag{1}
$$

The following picture shows the point $(\cosh \phi,\ \sinh \phi)$ on unit hyperbola,
where $\phi$ is hyperbolic angle and the area of red sector represent $\phi/2$.

![Hyperbola](/assets/image/Hyperbolic_functions.png){: .align-center }

Since an infinitesimal change to the
red area is

$$
dA = \frac{1}{2}\left( x\,dy - y\,dx \right) \tag{2}
$$

If we differentiate equation (1) by $\phi$ and divide equation(2) by $d\phi$,
then we can see $\cosh \phi$ and $\sinh \phi$ are the solutions to the system of two ODEs.

$$
\begin{cases}
x x' - y y' = 0 \\
1 = x y' - y x'
\end{cases}
$$

after algebraic eliminations it gives us $x' = y, y' = x$, in other words, $y'' = y$, which is a
linear homogeneous constant-coefficient second-order ODE. To solve it, try $y = e^{rt}$ to calculate its characteristic equation, which is $r^2 = 1$, so the
general solution is:

$$
y(t) = C_1 e^t + C_2 e^{-t}
$$

pass the intial value that $\cosh(0) = 1$ and $\sinh(0) = 0$, we can obtain the value to be

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cosh \phi &= \frac{e^\phi + e^{-\phi}}{2} \\[6pt]
\sinh \phi &= \frac{e^\phi - e^{-\phi}}{2} \\[6pt]
\tanh \phi &= \frac{\sinh \phi}{\cosh \phi}
= \frac{e^\phi - e^{-\phi}}{e^\phi + e^{-\phi}}
\end{aligned}
$$
