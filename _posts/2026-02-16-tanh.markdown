Why tanh is defined as following?

$$
\tanh(x) = \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{e^x + e^{-x}}
$$

### What's hyperbola?

A hyperbola is the set of all points where the absolute difference of distances to two fixed points (foci) is constant.

$$
\lVert PF_1 - PF_2 \rVert = constant
$$

base on this definition and after a tedious algebraic expansion and elimination, you can obtain a nice analytic form.

$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
$$

For example, this is the graph of unit hyperbola $x^2 -y^2 = 1$.

### What's hyperbolic function?

In 1748 [^intro-infinite] Euler discovered the famous Euler's formula:

$$
e^{ix} = \cos x + i \sin x
$$

, which can be proved by power series:

$$
\begin{aligned}
e^x &= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!} \\[6pt]
\cos x &= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!} \\[6pt]
\sin x &= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}
\end{aligned}
$$

, from Euler's formula, we can derive the exponential definition of $\sin$ and $\cos$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos x &= \frac{e^{ix} + e^{-ix}}{2} \\[6pt]
\sin x &= \frac{e^{ix} - e^{-ix}}{2i}
\end{aligned}
$$

, from which it's easy to see
$\cos^2 \theta + \sin^2 \theta = 1$, so point $(\cos \theta,\ \sin \theta)$  is on the unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, hence match our geometric intuition.


In modern math, hyperbolic functions are usually directly defined in exponential form similar to above.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cosh x &= \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2} \\[6pt]
\sinh x &= \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{2} \\[6pt]
\tanh x &= \frac{\sinh x}{\cosh x}
= \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{e^x + e^{-x}}
\end{aligned}
$$

with this explict definition, it is easy to verify that $\cosh^2 \phi - \sinh^2 \phi = 1$, so point $(\cosh \phi,\ \sinh \phi)$  is on the unit hyperbola $x^2 - y^2 = 1$, hence the name $\sinh$ for hyperbolic sine.

It's also easy to verify that $\sinh$ and $\cosh$ is the solution to ODE $y'' = y$, so are any combination of these two, i.e. $y = A \cosh \phi + B \sinh \phi$.
