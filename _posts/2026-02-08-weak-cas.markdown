Compare-and-set (CAS) and load-link/store-conditional (LL/SC) are two flavors of CPU
instructions for atomic read-modify-write operations. CPUs may provide either or both
of them.

In C++, `compare_exchange_strong` (Strong CAS) is compiled into CAS instruction if exists, otherwise it uses what CPU gives to mimic the behavior, and
`compare_exchange_weak` (Weak CAS) is compiled into LL/SC if exists, otherwise it uses
what CPU gives to mimic the behavior.

A study [^measurement] shows LL/SC outperforms CAS consistently in a range of ARM
CPUs.
CAS is also subjected to ABA problem if naively implemented while LL/SC is much better
[^ABA].
It may explain why `compare_exchange_weak` is preferred in [jemalloc][weak-jemalloc].

[^measurement]: Bastos Cordeiro de Jesus, RJ & Weiland, M 2023, A study on the performance implications of AArch64 Atomics. in ISC High Performance 2023. Springer, pp. 279-296, ISC High Performance 2023, Hamburg, Germany, 21/05/23.

[^ABA]: https://blog.memzero.de/cas-llsc-aba/

[weak-jemalloc]: https://github.com/search?q=repo%3Ajemalloc%2Fjemalloc%20compare_exchange_weak&type=code
